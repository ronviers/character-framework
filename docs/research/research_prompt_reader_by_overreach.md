# Research prompt — READER-BY-OVERREACH + the offline-read corollary: defer-or-claim

**For the outbound multi-model research channel.** Self-contained; assumes no prior context. **Constraint:
open-access / freely-downloadable sources only** (open-access journals, author/lab sites, PMC, arXiv/bioRxiv,
philsci-archive, public repos). Return a unified, source-cited report.

**This is a deferral audit, not a discovery hunt.** We have a conjecture and several candidate readings. We need
to know **where each already lives in the established literature**, so we *import and cite by name* rather than
reinvent. "This is exactly Still / England / McClelland / Pattee / Eigen — here is the citation and the precise
correspondence" is the **most valuable** outcome, not a disappointing one. We want to be told what to defer to,
and to have any residue sized honestly and small.

## Background object (what the framework already has)

A driven non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) has a finite **keep** — the organization it recovers through
complete turnover of its parts — bounded by a **wall**: maintaining a heterogeneous wiring of `N` loops costs
~`N log N` to re-specify while the running dynamics repair only ~`N` per unit dissipation. Past the wall, surplus
organization must be **archived** (a standing record the dynamics consult but cannot regenerate). A **reader** is
the machinery that reconstructs the running organization from the archive. (Already imported: archive-held term =
Kolchinsky–Wolpert stored semantic information; reader-produces-reader seam = Rosen closure to efficient
causation / Pattee epistemic cut.)

## The conjecture and corollary (what we want located)

**Reader-by-overreach.** Near the keep's ceiling, a system that repeatedly *overreaches* — transiently closing
loops more heterogeneous than its turnover can re-specify — runs briefly more efficiently, then fails when
re-specification beats repair. Each failure deposits a persistent **trace** (a metric/configurational residue).
Once *consulting the trace costs less dissipation than re-minting the loop from scratch, including the reader's
own upkeep*, readout machinery is thermodynamically selected — producing the **reader**. Path:
ascent → failure → trace → archive → reader.

**The offline-read corollary.** A keep near its ceiling cannot spend all dissipation maintaining organization
*and* build/use the reader at once (finite budget; "near the ceiling" = repair ≈ capacity); and reading traces
masked by the running attractor may require *loosening* that attractor (a controlled descent into the metric
sector). So **offline reconstruction phases** (exploit → consolidate → exploit) are predicted as structural,
with a falsifiable signature: **anti-correlation between running coordination and reading**.

## The questions (ranked) — for each: the precise formal claim, the citation, the closest existing computation, and an explicit verdict (owns it / partial / doesn't)

1. **The crossover.** Is "a system stores information precisely when consulting it costs less than recomputing
   it, and readout/predictive machinery is thermodynamically *selected* for that saving" owned? Candidates:
   **Still et al., "Thermodynamics of Prediction"** (PRL 2012) + Still's later nonequilibrium-prediction work;
   **England, dissipative adaptation** (J. Chem. Phys. 2013; Perunov–Marsland–England, PRX 2016); **Bennett,
   logical depth** (1988) / thermodynamics of computation. Does *any* tie the saving to a **maintenance /
   error-correction wall** (re-specification outrunning repair) rather than prediction-in-general? Closest thing
   to "a *reader* selected by a dissipation crossover"?

2. **The trace.** Is "a driven system pushed past its maintenance capacity leaves an *informative, re-readable*
   residue — one that lowers the cost of re-reaching the prior configuration" established? Candidates: return-point
   / hysteresis memory (Sethna; Preisach), aging in glasses, eligibility traces, reservoir computing, synaptic
   tagging-and-capture. Established result on whether such residues carry *usable mutual information* with the
   configuration that produced them, vs. entropic noise? (This is the conjecture's weakest assumption — press it.)

3. **The forced alternation.** Is "a system that both runs and must reorganize from a record is *forced* to
   time-share into online/offline phases" owned? Candidates: **Complementary Learning Systems**
   (McClelland–McNaughton–O'Reilly 1995); **Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis** (Tononi–Cirelli); the **Wake-Sleep
   algorithm** (Hinton et al. 1995); **experience replay** (RL); the **stability–plasticity dilemma** (Grossberg);
   **Crick–Mitchison** reverse-learning & **Hopfield** "unlearning" (1983); **simulated annealing**. For each:
   functional/empirical, or is there a *thermodynamic (dissipation-budget)* derivation that offline phases are
   *necessary*? Sharpest antecedent for **"reading requires loosening the running attractor"**? Anyone reporting
   the predicted **coordination ⊥ reading anti-correlation**?

4. **The form of the archive — forced, or frozen accident?** (a) Is there a named, established argument that
   high-fidelity heritable storage is *pressed toward* discrete/digital, error-correctable encoding as the
   information it must carry grows? Candidates: **von Neumann** digital restoration / reliable computation from
   unreliable parts; **Shannon** channel coding; **Eigen's error threshold** (sustainable sequence complexity
   bounded by per-symbol copy fidelity); **Pattee's** rate-independence of the symbol. State each precisely.
   (b) **Crucially** — is the *specific* realized form (a **digital, aperiodic 1-D polymer**) regarded as
   **forced/convergent** or as a **frozen accident** (Crick; Gould's contingency)? With a single realized origin
   (`n = 1`), is necessity-vs-contingency even *decidable*? We want the established position on what is a genuine
   selective pressure (a gradient) versus contingent baggage read backward as necessity. (Place Pattee's cut at
   the **archive/keep** = genotype/phenotype boundary, *not* any within-dynamics split.)

5. **The residue.** After 1–4, what (if anything) is *not* in the literature? We suspect the framework's only
   originals are (a) anchoring the crossover specifically to the **keep's maintenance wall** (~`N log N` vs ~`N`),
   and (b) a **sector reading** — the reader as a metric→topological back-channel — that *predicts* a measured
   biological observation (JCVI-Syn3A: the genome's irreducible archive is dominated by the expression/reader
   apparatus, ≈41% of archive bits, where the circulation/archive cut blurs). Confirm or shrink: is even (a) or
   (b) already present?

6. **Disconfirmers.** What would *kill* the conjecture: a result that failed-overreach traces are generically
   uninformative; that offline reconstruction is demonstrably *not* budget-forced (routine concurrent online
   reorganization); or that the crossover dies once the reader's own maintenance cost is included.

## Output

A unified report, every claim source-cited (open-access link), organized by the six questions. For each candidate

End with a one-paragraph **residue statement**: the smallest honest description of what, if anything, is the
framework's own, at its true (small) size — and, for Q4, a one-line verdict on whether "the archive had to be
digital/aperiodic" is a defensible necessity, a bounded selective pressure, or an `n = 1` frozen accident.

### Q1. The Crossover: "Consulting costs less than recomputing → readout machinery thermodynamically selected"

**Still et al., "Thermodynamics of Prediction" (PRL 2012)**  
*One-line formal claim:* A system responding to stochastic driving computes an implicit predictive model of its environment; the non-predictive (model-inefficient) fraction of retained information is fundamentally equivalent to thermodynamic inefficiency, measured by dissipation. Any system constructed to keep memory and operate with maximal energetic efficiency must be predictive.  
*Open access:* [arXiv:1203.3271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3271)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Still et al. establish the equivalence between predictive information and thermodynamic efficiency, and show that memory retention is tied to dissipation costs. However, they do **not** tie this to a *maintenance wall* where re-specification outruns repair. Their framework is about prediction-in-general, not about a system pushed past its capacity to regenerate its own organization. The "crossover" they describe is between predictive and non-predictive information, not between regeneration cost and readout cost. The *reader* as a distinct entity selected by a dissipation crossover at a maintenance ceiling is **not present**.

**England, dissipative adaptation (J. Chem. Phys. 2013; Perunov–Marsland–England, PRX 2016; Nature Nanotech 2015 perspective)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Driven many-body systems statistically prefer states with histories of atypically high work absorption and dissipation; structures that are better at absorbing and dissipating work from a particular drive become irreversibly "stuck" in the configuration space, appearing self-organized and "well adapted" to environmental conditions.  
*Open access:* [England perspective PDF](https://englandlab.com/uploads/7/8/0/3/7803054/nnano.2015.250__1_.pdf)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** England's mechanism is *ascent via dissipation* — systems get stuck in high-dissipation configurations. This is the "ascent" half of your path. But England does **not** address the *failure* half: what happens when a system transiently overreaches and cannot re-specify its loops. There is no "trace → archive → reader" trajectory. The maintenance wall (~N log N vs ~N) is absent. Dissipative adaptation explains why structures persist, not why they *must* archive when they can no longer regenerate.

**Bennett, logical depth / thermodynamics of computation (1988)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Logical depth measures the computational work required to produce an object from a near-random initial state; thermodynamically deep objects require significant dissipation to compute and are correspondingly hard to reverse.  
**Verdict: DOESN'T.** Bennett's framework concerns the thermodynamic cost of *computing* structures, not the cost of *maintaining* them against turnover, nor the selection of readout machinery. The maintenance/repair wall is absent.

**Kolchinsky–Wolpert, "Semantic Information, Autonomous Agency and Non-Equilibrium Statistical Physics" (Interface Focus 2018)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Semantic information is the syntactic information a physical system has about its environment that is *causally necessary* for the system to maintain its own existence (low-entropy state); the "value of information" is the viability difference between actual and intervened (scrambled) distributions.  
*Open access:* [Royal Society](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsfs/article/8/6/20180041/64255/Semantic-information-autonomous-agency-and-non)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Kolchinsky–Wolpert define semantic information in terms of causal necessity for self-maintenance, and introduce the "value of information" as a viability difference. This is conceptually adjacent to your "crossover" — information is "semantic" precisely when losing it costs viability. However, they do **not** derive a *reader* from this: their framework identifies which information is meaningful, not how a *machine* to read it is thermodynamically selected. The maintenance wall and the overreach→trace→reader path are absent. Their "stored semantic information" is the archive-held term you already import; their framework does not predict the reader as a separate entity.

**Closest thing to "a reader selected by a dissipation crossover":** None. The pieces exist separately — Still (prediction↔dissipation), England (ascent via dissipation), Kolchinsky–Wolpert (semantic information as viability-relevant) — but no one assembles them into: *a system pushed past regeneration capacity deposits traces, and when consulting traces costs less than re-minting, readout machinery is selected*. This assembly is the framework's candidate original.

### Q2. The Trace: "Informative, re-readable residue from overreach"

**Return-point / hysteresis memory (Sethna; Preisach model)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Hysteretic systems retain memory of their magnetic/economic history in the form of return-point memory — the state depends on the path taken through phase space, not just the current parameters.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Return-point memory establishes that driven systems leave *persistent configurational residues* that encode history. However, the literature focuses on whether these residues carry *usable mutual information* with the prior configuration versus being mere entropic noise. Sethna's work (e.g., *Statistical Mechanics: Entropy, Order Parameters, and Complexity*) treats hysteresis as a *collective* phenomenon where the memory is distributed across many degrees of freedom. There is no established result that a single overreach event deposits a *localized, high-fidelity* trace that lowers the cost of re-reaching the prior configuration. The "informative" vs "entropic noise" distinction is precisely the gap.

**Aging in glasses / amorphous solids**  
*One-line formal claim:* Structural glasses age via spontaneous and driven relaxation processes that leave the system in progressively deeper energy minima; the aging trajectory encodes thermal history.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Glass aging demonstrates that out-of-equilibrium systems retain *history-dependent* configurational states. But the residues are typically treated as *disordered* — the system remembers that it was driven, not *how* to reconstruct a specific prior state. The mutual information between the current aged state and the specific configuration that produced it is generally assumed to be *lost* to mixing, not preserved as a readable trace.

**Synaptic tagging-and-capture (Frey & Morris 1997; subsequent work)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Late-phase LTP requires a "tag" set by early-phase LTP at the same synapse, plus capture of plasticity-related proteins (PRPs) synthesized in the cell body; the tag is a local, persistent molecular mark that enables consolidation.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** This is a *biological* instance of trace formation: a transient event (early LTP) deposits a persistent molecular tag that enables later reconstruction (late LTP) when PRPs are available. The tag is "readable" in the sense that it gates protein capture. However, this is a *specific molecular mechanism*, not a general thermodynamic result about driven systems. The connection to a *general* "overreach→trace" principle for arbitrary NESSs is not established.

**Reservoir computing / echo state networks**  
*One-line formal claim:* Dynamical systems with fading memory can serve as reservoirs for computation; the reservoir's state at time t encodes a nonlinear transformation of input history, readable by a linear readout layer.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Reservoir computing demonstrates that driven dynamical systems can retain *readable* traces of past inputs in their current state. But the "trace" here is the *entire reservoir state*, not a localized residue from a specific overreach event. The framework assumes the reservoir is *designed* to have fading memory and separable states; it does not derive trace formation from a maintenance wall.

**Eligibility traces (RL; Sutton & Barto)**  
*One-line formal claim:* In temporal-difference learning, an eligibility trace is a short-term memory of recent state visits that decays exponentially, enabling credit assignment over time.  
**Verdict: DOESN'T.** Eligibility traces are algorithmic constructs for credit assignment; they are not physical residues from overreach, nor is their informativeness derived from thermodynamics.

**Established result on usable mutual information:** There is **no** established general result that overreach residues carry *usable* mutual information with the configuration that produced them, as opposed to entropic noise. This is the conjecture's **weakest assumption**, and the literature does not own it. The closest are specific biological cases (synaptic tagging) and engineered cases (reservoir computing), but neither generalizes to arbitrary NESSs.

### Q3. The Forced Alternation: "Online/offline phases are structural; coordination ⊥ reading anti-correlation"

**Complementary Learning Systems (McClelland–McNaughton–O'Reilly 1995; Norman & O'Reilly 2003)**  
*One-line formal claim:* The hippocampus rapidly memorizes specific episodes via pattern separation, while neocortex slowly learns statistical regularities via overlapping representations; this division avoids catastrophic interference in cortical learning.  
*Open access:* [Princeton PDF](https://www.princeton.edu/~compmem/psyrev_inpress.pdf)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** CLS is a *functional* argument for why two systems with different learning rates must coexist. It is **not** a thermodynamic derivation that offline phases are *necessary* due to a dissipation budget. The "forced alternation" in CLS arises from the *computational* need to avoid interference, not from a finite dissipation budget where running and reorganizing cannot occur simultaneously. The anti-correlation between running coordination and reading is not predicted.

**Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (Tononi–Cirelli; Sleep 2014 review)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Sleep is the price the brain pays for plasticity — wake causes net synaptic potentiation (increasing energy cost, decreasing S/N, saturating learning), while sleep renormalizes synaptic strength via down-selection, restoring homeostasis and enabling new learning.  
*Open access:* [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3921176/)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** SHY is the **sharpest antecedent** for "offline phases are necessary." It provides a *cellular and systems-level* rationale: potentiation in wake is energetically expensive and saturates learning; renormalization in sleep is required to restore capacity. However, SHY's argument is *biological*, not *thermodynamic* in the sense of a general NESS dissipation budget. The "necessity" is derived from cellular energy costs, signal-to-noise degradation, and the plasticity-selectivity dilemma — not from a general ~N log N vs ~N maintenance wall. The predicted anti-correlation between running coordination (wake) and reading/consolidation (sleep) is **empirically supported** (SWA correlates with prior wake plasticity; local sleep follows local wake plasticity), but the *thermodynamic* derivation is absent.

**Wake-Sleep algorithm (Hinton et al. 1995)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Unsupervised learning in neural networks proceeds via alternating wake phases (recognition weights updated to match generative model) and sleep phases (generative weights updated to match recognition model).  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** This is an *algorithmic* alternation, not a thermodynamic necessity. The sleep phase in Hinton's algorithm is for updating the generative model, not for "reading traces masked by the running attractor." The connection to dissipation budgets is absent.

**Experience replay (RL; Dyna algorithms; Sutton 1991)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Offline replay of past experiences enables planning and policy improvement without new environmental interaction; it offloads computational costs to periods not occupied by task behavior.  
*Open access:* [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303108/)  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Experience replay demonstrates that offline phases are *functionally useful* for learning. The "offloading" argument is adjacent to your budget argument. But this is computational, not thermodynamic. The necessity of *loosening the running attractor* to read traces is not present.

**Stability–plasticity dilemma (Grossberg 1987)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Neural networks face a fundamental trade-off: plasticity is needed to learn new patterns, but destabilizes previously learned ones; stability requires limiting plasticity.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** This is the *computational* analog of your dissipation-budget argument. Grossberg identifies the dilemma but resolves it via algorithmic mechanisms (e.g., adaptive resonance theory), not via a thermodynamic derivation that offline phases are forced by a finite budget.

**Crick–Mitchison reverse learning / Hopfield "unlearning" (1983)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Sleep may serve to unlearn spurious associations accumulated during wake, preventing parasitic modes from dominating network dynamics.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** The "unlearning" proposal is functionally similar to your "loosening the running attractor" — removing spurious correlations to enable better organization. But again, this is a *computational* proposal, not a thermodynamic derivation. The anti-correlation prediction is absent.

**Simulated annealing**  
*One-line formal claim:* Optimization requires alternating high-temperature phases (exploration, escaping local minima) and low-temperature phases (exploitation, settling into good minima).  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** The alternation structure is analogous, but simulated annealing is an *algorithmic* schedule, not a thermodynamic necessity for a NESS. The "reading" phase is not identified with consulting an archive.

**Sharpest antecedent for "reading requires loosening the running attractor":** The **Crick–Mitchison/Hopfield** "unlearning" proposal is the closest functional analog, but it lacks the thermodynamic derivation. **SHY** is the closest empirical system with the predicted anti-correlation (wake potentiation ↔ sleep down-selection), but its necessity argument is biological, not general-thermodynamic.

**Anyone reporting coordination ⊥ reading anti-correlation?** Yes — SHY reports that slow-wave activity (a marker of sleep-dependent renormalization) anti-correlates with prior wake plasticity and correlates with post-sleep performance improvement. Local sleep (decreased SWA) follows local inactivity (e.g., arm immobilization), and increased SWA follows local plasticity (e.g., motor learning). This is a **measured anti-correlation between running coordination and offline reading/consolidation**, but it is empirical, not derived from a thermodynamic budget.

### Q4. The Form of the Archive: Forced or Frozen Accident?

**(a) Discrete/digital, error-correctable encoding pressed by growing information load**

**von Neumann, "Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis of Reliable Organisms from Unreliable Components" (1952 lectures; Automata Studies 1956)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Reliable computation can be achieved from unreliable components via massive redundancy (multiplexing) and restorative organs; error correction is necessary for reliable information processing in noisy substrates.  
*Open access:* [MIT PDF](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/862.16/notes/computation/vonNeumann-1956.pdf)  
**Verdict: OWNS IT (partially).** Von Neumann established that reliable information processing from unreliable parts requires error correction. However, his argument is about *computation*, not about *heritable storage* per se. The connection to the specific form (digital, aperiodic 1-D polymer) is not made.

**Shannon, channel coding theorem (1948)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Reliable communication over a noisy channel is possible at rates below channel capacity via appropriate coding; error-correcting codes can achieve arbitrarily low error probability.  
**Verdict: OWNS IT (partially).** Shannon established the theoretical possibility of error-correctable digital encoding. But this is general information theory; the connection to biological heritable storage and the specific form of the archive is not made.

**Eigen's error threshold (quasispecies theory; 1971 onward)**  
*One-line formal claim:* The maximum sustainable sequence complexity (information content) of a replicator is bounded by the per-symbol copy fidelity; above the error threshold, genetic information is lost to mutation accumulation.  
*Open access:* [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5492144/)  
**Verdict: OWNS IT (partially).** Eigen's error threshold is the **sharpest antecedent** for the claim that high-fidelity heritable storage is *pressed toward* error-correctable encoding as information load grows. It directly ties information capacity to copy fidelity. However, Eigen's framework concerns *replication fidelity*, not the *specific physical form* (digital polymer) of the archive. The "digital" aspect is assumed, not derived.

**Pattee's rate-independence of the symbol**  
*One-line formal claim:* The essential feature of a symbol is its rate-independence — the symbol must be conserved while the dynamics it controls vary arbitrarily; this requires a separation between the symbolic (slow, conserved) and dynamic (fast, variable) aspects of a system.  
**Verdict: PARTIALLY.** Pattee's rate-independence is conceptually adjacent to your archive/keep distinction — the archive is rate-independent relative to the dynamics. However, Pattee does **not** derive the *digital, aperiodic polymer* form from this. His "epistemic cut" is placed between symbol and matter, not specifically at the genotype/phenotype boundary as you propose.

**(b) Is the specific realized form (digital, aperiodic 1-D polymer) forced/convergent or frozen accident?**

**Crick's "frozen accident" (1968)**  
*One-line formal claim:* The genetic code is universal because any change would be lethal; the original allocation of codons to amino acids was a matter of "chance" that became frozen by the integration of the code into cellular machinery.  
*Open access:* [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5492144/)  
**Verdict: OWNS THE "FROZEN ACCIDENT" POSITION.** Crick explicitly argued that the code's form is contingent, not forced. The universality is due to common descent, not convergence.

**Gould's contingency thesis (1989; Wonderful Life)**  
*One-line formal claim:* Evolutionary outcomes are profoundly sensitive to historical contingencies; replaying life's tape would yield radically different results.  
*Open access:* [Science 2018 review](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aam5979)  
**Verdict: OWNS THE CONTINGENCY POSITION.** Gould's n=1 problem is directly relevant: with only one realized origin, necessity vs. contingency is undecidable. However, recent work on convergent evolution challenges the radical contingency thesis, suggesting that some outcomes are robustly replicable.

**The n=1 problem and decidability**  
The literature is **explicit** that with a single realized origin (n=1), necessity vs. contingency is **not decidable** in the strong sense. As noted in the convergent evolution literature: "We are currently privvy to a single history of life, and thus we are unable to investigate the stability of large-scale evolutionary outcomes in this way" . Gould's thought experiments are designed to circumvent this, but "such exercises of the imagination... are ultimately speculative and empirically inconclusive" .

**Recent developments challenging pure frozen accident:** The 2024 arXiv paper on "The Genetic Code Paradox" notes that engineered code changes in *E. coli* (Syn61) are survivable, challenging the "frozen" aspect, but the extreme conservation across 3.5 billion years remains paradoxical . The 2011 Philip & Freeland paper found that life's amino acid alphabet outperforms random alternatives in coverage of chemical properties, suggesting *some* selective optimization . Koonin's 2009 review argues that frozen accident, HGT requirements, and error minimization are complementary, not exclusive .

**One-line verdict on "the archive had to be digital/aperiodic":**  
**Bounded selective pressure, not defensible necessity.** Eigen's error threshold and von Neumann's reliable-computation arguments establish that *some* form of error-correctable encoding is strongly selected for as information load grows. However, the *specific* realization as a digital, aperiodic 1-D polymer (DNA/RNA) is best regarded as a **frozen accident** with **bounded selective optimization** — the general requirement (discrete, error-correctable) is forced; the particular molecular implementation is contingent on historical origin. With n=1, stronger claims are unwarranted.

### Q5. The Residue: What (if anything) is not in the literature?

After auditing Q1–Q4, the following appears to be **not owned** by the established literature:

**(a) Anchoring the crossover specifically to the keep's maintenance wall (~N log N vs ~N).**  
This is **not present**. Still et al. tie dissipation to predictive information, but not to a *maintenance wall* where re-specification cost scales as N log N while repair scales as N. England's dissipative adaptation explains ascent but not the ceiling. No existing work derives a "reader" from the condition that *consulting the trace costs less dissipation than re-minting the loop from scratch, including the reader's own upkeep*, specifically at the point where repair ≈ capacity.

**(b) Sector reading — the reader as a metric→topological back-channel predicting JCVI-Syn3A's genome architecture.**  
This is **not present**. The observation that JCVI-Syn3A's minimal genome dedicates ~58% of protein-coding genes to "Genetic Information Processing" (DNA maintenance, folding/sorting/degradation, transcription, translation)  — with translation alone at ~39.5% of genes — is an *empirical fact*. The *interpretation* that this represents the irreducible archive being dominated by the expression/reader apparatus, where the circulation/archive cut blurs, is a **novel theoretical framing**. The literature reports the numbers but does not place them in a "reader-by-overreach" framework.

**Confirm or shrink:** Both (a) and (b) appear to be **genuinely original** to the framework, at least in their specific combination. Individual components (maintenance costs, error thresholds, semantic information, minimal genomes) are established, but their assembly into the "reader-by-overreach" narrative with the maintenance wall as the selection pressure is not.

### Q6. Disconfirmers: What Would Kill the Conjecture?

1. **Failed-overreach traces are generically uninformative.**  
   If experiments or simulations show that when a driven system transiently exceeds its maintenance capacity, the resulting residues carry *no usable mutual information* with the prior configuration — i.e., they are pure entropic noise, not readable traces — then the trace→archive→reader path collapses. The glass-aging and hysteresis literature suggests residues *do* carry some history-dependence, but whether this is *informative* (in the sense of lowering re-reach cost) is the critical question.

2. **Offline reconstruction is demonstrably not budget-forced.**  
   If a system is shown to routinely perform *concurrent* online reorganization from a record without time-sharing — i.e., it maintains full running coordination while simultaneously reading and reorganizing from archive — then the "forced alternation" prediction fails. The biological evidence (sleep, SHY) strongly supports alternation, but a counter-example from a non-biological NESS would be damaging.

3. **The crossover dies once the reader's own maintenance cost is included.**  
   If accounting for the thermodynamic cost of building and maintaining the reader machinery makes "consulting the trace" *more* expensive than "re-minting from scratch" in all regimes, then the thermodynamic selection argument for the reader fails. This is an *a priori* falsifiable calculation: one can estimate the dissipation cost of reader construction/maintenance versus loop re-specification and ask whether a crossover region exists.

## Residue Statement

The framework's honest residue, at its true (small) size, is:

> **(a) A specific maintenance wall** — the claim that maintaining heterogeneous wiring of N loops costs ~N log N to re-specify while dynamics repair only ~N per unit dissipation, and that this wall forces a crossover where consulting traces becomes cheaper than regeneration. The scaling argument itself is not novel (it echoes combinatorial complexity bounds), but its application as the *thermodynamic selection pressure for a reader* is not found in the literature.
>
> **(b) Sector reading** — the reader as a metric→topological back-channel that predicts the architecture of minimal genomes (JCVI-Syn3A: ~58% genetic information processing, with the circulation/archive cut blurred). The empirical data exist; the theoretical framing as a prediction of reader-by-overreach does not.

Everything else is deferrable: the crossover concept is Still + England + Kolchinsky–Wolpert (assembled differently); the trace concept is hysteresis + synaptic tagging + reservoir computing (generalized); the forced alternation is CLS + SHY + wake-sleep algorithms (thermodynamicized); the archive form is von Neumann + Shannon + Eigen + Pattee (placed at a specific boundary).

**Q4 one-line verdict:** "The archive had to be digital/aperiodic" is a **bounded selective pressure** — error-correctable discrete encoding is strongly selected for as information load grows (Eigen, von Neumann), but the specific molecular realization as a 1-D aperiodic polymer is a **frozen accident** (Crick) with **n=1 undecidability** (Gould). Stronger necessity claims are not defensible.
